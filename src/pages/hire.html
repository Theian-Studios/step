What participants bring

Skills built on real work.

Participants spend each week on detail-heavy records research for a BYU research lab, working remotely and managing their own schedule. The work builds skills that carry into most jobs.

  • Working with records and databases
  • Excel and other everyday tools
  • Professional writing
  • Careful, detail-oriented research
  • Remote, self-directed work
  • Setting and keeping a consistent schedule

Look past the gap

A track record of showing up

Participants keep a consistent weekly schedule, complete weekly training, and check in regularly with our team, so they come to you with a record of reliable work.

Recent, explainable work history

Every participant has recent, paid experience on a large research project hosted by BYU’s Record Linking Lab, and a clear answer to “What have you been doing lately?”

Questions

Hiring FAQ

What kinds of roles are participants a good fit for?

It depends on the person. Participants come from very different backgrounds, from construction and machining to family history work. In the program, all of them build experience with remote work, records research, and everyday software. Tell us about your role and we’ll let you know if someone may be a fit.

How do we get started?

Send us a short message about the position using our contact form and choose “Hiring participants.” We’ll follow up with you.

Who runs the program?

STEP provides the jobs, training, and support, and donations pay participants’ wages. The work itself is a family history research project hosted by BYU’s Department of Economics and its Record Linking Lab in Provo.
