About us

A first step toward stable work.

STEP, the Supporting Transitions to Employment Program, offers paid, meaningful work to people experiencing homelessness in Utah County and helps preserve family connections for future generations.

Breaking the cycle

The barriers start before the first day of work.

Imagine receiving a job offer—but being unable to accept it because you don’t own a pair of work boots. Or because your identification was stolen. Or because you can’t afford the bus fare to get there. Perhaps you never receive a job offer at all because your resume has a gap explained only by the label “homeless.”

For thousands of people experiencing homelessness, these aren’t hypothetical situations. They are everyday realities.

Employment is one of the surest paths out of homelessness, but finding and keeping a steady job is a circle with no way in. Employers expect:

  • References
  • Reliable transportation
  • Clean clothing
  • A clean record
  • Government-issued identification
  • A reliable phone connection
  • A permanent address

And without a job, none of that is within reach.

Building opportunity

We meet people where they are.

STEP was founded in 2026 to help break that cycle. Participants work on a family history research project hosted by Brigham Young University’s Department of Economics: the university hosts the research, and STEP provides the jobs, training, and support. {{ site.participants_so_far }} people have worked in the program so far. It offers flexible, remote work where participants build skills, regain confidence, and get help with the things standing between them and a long-term job.

We don’t expect anyone to overcome every barrier of homelessness before they can work. Instead, we designed the job around those challenges rather than waiting for them to be solved.

Fully remote. Participants without reliable transportation can complete assignments anywhere with internet access: a public library, a university campus, a shelter, or even a phone.

10 hours a week. Many participants are balancing housing instability, medical appointments, or other personal challenges, so participants work only 10 hours a week on a schedule they set themselves. That provides flexibility while building consistency and responsibility.

Weekly training. Participants also receive weekly training that builds technology skills, strengthens workplace confidence, and gives them recent, explainable work history.

A lasting impact

Two goals that outlast the program.

First, we help participants secure long-term employment by building meaningful work experience, developing transferable skills, and overcoming the barriers that keep people out of work.

Second, participants leave something behind for other families. Their work with BYU’s Record Linking Lab helps preserve family connections for future generations. See what the lab does.
