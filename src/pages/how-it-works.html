How it works

From a first application to a stable future.

STEP works in three stages. Here’s what happens from the day someone applies to the day they move on to their next job.

1

Step 1: Start working

Getting in the door and earning from week one.

Apply

People experiencing homelessness apply to join the program, often after hearing about it from a friend or someone who’s already come through it. No resume or spotless work history required—just a willingness to show up and try.

Onboard

The first month lays the groundwork for employment. Participants complete weekly training, check in regularly with our team, and start earning wages right away.

12

Step 2: Build skills and stability

Steady, paid work and the routine that comes with it.

Work

Participants take on consistent, paid work: 10 hours a week of records research alongside BYU economics students in the Record Linking Lab. What the work involves is explained below.

Employment

After the first month, participants are hired as part-time employees. They keep building skills that carry well beyond this project: working with records and databases, using Excel and other everyday tools, professional writing, and keeping a consistent schedule.

Stabilize

Participants work remotely for a set period, earning steady pay. It’s a foothold that unlocks a lot: even a modest, consistent income helps secure housing, a working phone, hygiene items, and transportation.

Step 3: Move on to a lasting job

Ready for what comes after STEP.

Move forward

Our goal is not to keep participants in the program indefinitely, but to prepare them for opportunities beyond it. Through meaningful work experience, professional development, and assistance obtaining essential documentation, participants are equipped to pursue stable, higher-paying employment.

The work

What the Record Linking Lab does

The BYU Record Linking Lab, part of the Department of Economics, builds tools that link families and individuals across historical records. Everything it does is meant to improve the Family Tree at FamilySearch.org, a free public tree that aims to connect everyone in the world, so more people can find their families.

The lab’s largest effort is the Census Tree: linking the 217 million people who lived in the United States between 1850 and 1940 across every census they appear in, and connecting each person to their parents, siblings, spouses, and children. When it’s finished, it will be the largest long-term data set ever created in the U.S., opening up new research in economics, demography, sociology, and public health.

The lab pairs machine learning, which reads and matches handwritten records quickly, with careful work by people, which catches what the software misses, especially for groups that are hard to link, like women and immigrants. STEP participants do that careful human work alongside more than 50 BYU students: merging duplicate records, adding people who are missing, cross-checking sources, and filling in details for people already on the tree.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Who can join the program?

STEP is for people experiencing homelessness in Utah County who want to work. You don’t need a resume, a recent job, a permanent address, or reliable transportation to apply. Many people hear about the program from a friend or from someone who has already been through it, and caseworkers and shelter staff can refer someone too.

Do participants need their own computer?

No. The work is fully remote and can be done anywhere with internet access, including public libraries, university campuses, shelters, or even a phone. Many participants work at the public library, where they sit near each other and help one another with questions.

How many hours do participants work?

About 10 hours a week, on a schedule each participant sets. Many are also searching for housing, going to medical appointments, or caring for family, so the hours are flexible while still building a steady routine.

What training do participants get?

Everyone takes part in weekly training. It covers the research work itself and skills that carry over to other jobs: working with records and databases, using Excel and other everyday software, professional writing, and keeping a consistent schedule. Participants also check in regularly with our team.

Are participants paid?

Yes, from the first day of onboarding. After the first month, participants are hired as part-time employees and keep earning steady pay. That income, and the proof of income that comes with it, is often what makes a housing application, a phone plan, or a bus pass possible.

How long do people stay in the program?

STEP is a stepping stone, not a permanent job. Participants work for a set period, long enough to build a recent work history and a steady routine. Along the way, our team helps them get essential documents like a photo ID and get ready to apply for stable, higher-paying work. Employers can hire participants directly when they’re ready.

What does the work actually involve?

Careful records research, done on a computer or phone. Participants find people who are missing from the FamilySearch Family Tree, merge duplicate records, cross-check details between historical sources, and fill in gaps for people already on the tree. They work alongside BYU students doing the same research. What the Record Linking Lab does covers it in full.

How can I refer someone?

Use our contact form and choose “Referring someone.” The form lists what to include, like how we can reach the person and a little about their situation. Caseworkers, shelter staff, friends, and family are all welcome to refer someone, as long as the person knows about STEP and has agreed to be referred.
